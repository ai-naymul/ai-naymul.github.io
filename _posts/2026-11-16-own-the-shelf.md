a supermarket spends decades selling you bread it did not bake. then one day it bakes its own.

## the shelf

about half of everything bought in a british supermarket now carries the supermarket's own name on
it. not the brand that spent fifty years perfecting the recipe. the shop.

the shop did not start by building a bakery. it started by owning the shelf.

the shelf is where a person decides. stand there long enough and you learn what sells, at what
price, on which day, to whom, and what they put back. then you make that thing yourself and keep
the margin.

it does not work in the other direction. a bakery cannot buy its way onto the shelf. it can only
ask to be allowed on.

## everyone wanted the bakery

when ai coding tools started working, there were three obvious things to build.

build a model trained only on code. the models were bad at code, so a specialist seemed certain to
win.

build an agent that writes the code for you.

build a plugin that sits inside the editor people already use. this one came with its own argument
attached. throwing out a whole editor to add one feature would be madness.

all three are reasonable. two of them are bakeries. the third is renting someone else's shelf.

## cursor forked the editor

they took the whole editor and made their own copy of it.

a plugin can only change what the editor allows a plugin to change. a fork can change anything. if
you believe the thing that matters is where the human meets the model, you cannot rent that place
from microsoft.

so they did not compete on models at all for a while. they used whatever frontier model was best
and spent their effort on the surface. the famous moment was not a clever architecture. it was
pressing tab and the right thing appearing.

## the shelf tells you things

every keystroke in that editor is a person deciding. accepted. rejected. edited and then accepted.
abandoned halfway.

nobody renting a shelf sees that. the model providers see a request and a response. the shop sees
the hand reaching out and stopping.

## then they built the bakery

in october 2025 cursor shipped its own model. then a second one. then a third.

and the detail i like most is that it is not a bakery from scratch. the newest one starts from
open weights somebody else trained and spends most of its compute on cursor's own training on top.

which is exactly what the supermarket does. tesco does not own wheat fields. it contracts a
manufacturer and brings the one thing nobody else has, which is knowing precisely what its own
shoppers reach for.

## you cannot run it backwards

a company that started by training a code model in 2023 had the bakery and no shelf. to get the
data that makes the model good it needed people using it all day. to get people using it all day
it needed to be where they already work. so it shipped a plugin, onto a shelf it did not own.

the sequence only runs one way. surface, then users, then data, then the model. skipping to the end
looks faster and arrives later.

## why i keep this

i split my weeks between research and building a product, and i keep having to decide which one
gets the time.

the shelf argument says that choice is not even. at perspectivity i already own a surface. every
reader who opens one version of a story and closes another is telling me something that no amount
of reading papers would. the pipeline behind it has taken in more than four hundred thousand
articles, but the articles are not the asset. the opening and the closing is.

so when i am choosing what to build next, the question is not which part is hardest or most
interesting. it is which part sits closest to somebody making a decision. that is the part that
teaches you what to build after it.

i get this wrong by instinct. the model work always feels more serious than the interface work. it
usually is not.
